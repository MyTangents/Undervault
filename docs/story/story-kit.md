# Sorcery: Realm of the Undervault. Story Kit

Reworkable story elements. Everything here is a part, not a plot. Tables have IDs (A3, C7) so recipes, events and generators can point at them. No game rules or numbers are defined here, only story.

---

## 1. Setting Bible

**The Undervault** is a hole that was dug on purpose. Nine hundred years ago a people called the **Lienholders of Sarn Oru** quarried straight down through the rock under what is now the town of **Crowhithe**, layer on layer, with ledges, bridges and drops the size of cathedrals. They left no bones, no portraits and no grave goods. They left ledgers. Every wall in the upper layers is scratched with columns of tally marks and a word nobody has fully translated, which scholars render as "owed."

**What the town thinks.** Crowhithe lives on the Undervault the way a gutter lives on rain. Adventurers arrive, buy things, die, and pay the temple to be less dead. The town calls the entrance shaft **the Gullet**. Parents tell children "the Vault is hungry," and then sell the children's older brothers a lantern.

**Voices and factions**
- **The Ledger Wardens.** Town council and tax men. They keep the **Roll of the Gone**, a very long book of everyone who went down and did not come up. They say it is for the families. It is also a debt book, and nobody has asked who they send the totals to.
- **The Sisters of the Ash.** Run the House of Ash (the temple). They believe a body is a loan and ash is the receipt. They are kind, tired and bad at bookkeeping, and they are the best friends a dead adventurer has.
- **The Dry Guild of Candlers.** Smiths, lampwrights and oil-sellers. A closed shop. They control light, so they control the descent, and they know it. Their motto is "Burn it slow."
- **The Tally-Singers.** A shabby cult of mud-lark scholars who hold that the Undervault is a debt, that each death pays a little of it down, and that when it is paid, something at the bottom will be released. They are either harmless or very wrong. They are cheerful about it.
- **The Quiet Below.** Not a faction. A voice, heard rarely, in the walls of deep layers, politely asking whether the party has "come about the account."

**The secret nobody knows.** The Lienholders did not build a tomb or a treasury. They built a **collateral vault**. They borrowed something enormous from something that lives at the bottom, and every layer is a payment held in trust: monsters are repossession agents, relics are pawned goods, and the dead of Crowhithe are installments. Leave open: who the creditor is, whether the debt is nearly paid, whether the Lienholders vanished or are still down there as the oldest, most patient things on the stairs, and what happens to a town whose economy is a payment plan.

## 2. Ingredient Tables

### A. Who You Meet in Town
Format: *Name, role. Quirk. Want.*

- **A1 Dennis Brannoch, recruiter at the Sump Tap.** Gives every recruit an invented heroic past and forgets which. Wants someone to call him "Brannoch the Bold" once, unironically.
- **A2 Sister Ottoline, ash-keeper at the House of Ash.** Weighs every urn by hand and says "light" or "heavy" with no explanation. Wants to know what the weight means.
- **A3 Hobb Ferrule, smith.** Names every blade and refuses to sell any he likes. Wants one customer to come back alive and return an unbroken sword.
- **A4 Maud the Lender, pawnbroker and informant.** Pays in tiny coins, wrapped in a new rumour each time. Wants a particular ring that pawned itself to her, in a manner she will not describe.
- **A5 Old Tamsin Quell, tale-teller at the Lamp and Lung.** Blind in one eye, tells every story as if she were there, and corrects people who say she wasn't. Wants someone to finish a story she cannot end.
- **A6 Perrin Lowe, a recruit: failed priest.** Heals competently, apologises to monsters. Wants to find the god who stopped answering and ask why.
- **A7 Gudrun Sixfingers, a recruit: fighter.** Fights with a bread knife on principle, to prove a point nobody remembers. Wants to die with a better weapon than she was born with.
- **A8 Thessaly Vane, a recruit: sorceress, nineteen, overqualified.** Corrects her own spells mid-cast, loudly. Wants the party to be a team of equals, which it cannot be.
- **A9 Corporal Aldric Moss, Ledger Warden clerk.** Reads out names from the Roll of the Gone at dawn, always one more than yesterday. Wants the Roll to stop growing, or at least to get a bigger book.
- **A10 Innkeeper Wenna Ashby, the Last Mattress.** Remembers every guest's last words if they are rude enough. Wants to be told one of her guests came back to say thanks.
- **A11 Joss the Sweeper, temple servant.** Sweeps ash into a jar and labels it by person. Wants someone to take a jar down and pour it where the person fell.
- **A12 Merrow Dunn, Tally-Singer in disguise as a candle seller.** Sells cheap tallow, sings softly while measuring. Wants the party to die in the right places, for the sums.
- **A13 Brother Kesk, an unsworn orc mercenary.** Knows all the dirty ways in and none of the clean ones. Wants his name taken off the Roll, where he is listed as dead, by a clerk's mistake.
- **A14 Little Pim, tavern boy.** Sells "authentic maps" drawn in ink by himself, sometimes right. Wants a real map to copy, to make better fakes.

### B. Places and Rooms in the Undervault
Format: *Place. Mood.*

- **B1 The Tallow Stair.** A spiral ramp slick with ancient candle fat, cut into a vast shaft. Greasy, claustrophobic, oddly warm.
- **B2 The Receiving Hall.** A banquet table forty paces long, set for guests who never came. Eerie hospitality, unspoilt wine.
- **B3 The Drop of Names.** A ledge above a shaft whose walls are scratched with tallies, thousands of them. Vertiginous, sad.
- **B4 The Choir Cistern.** A flooded cavern where rising water sings through old pipes. Beautiful and wrong.
- **B5 The Weighing Bridge.** A narrow span with a great balance at its centre that tilts under travellers. Tense, comic, deadly.
- **B6 The Pawn Gallery.** Row after row of glass cases of items with small paper tickets. Hushed, shop-like, creepy.
- **B7 The Slow Orchard.** A cavern of pale, bone-white trees bearing fruit that nobody has seen ripen. Hopeful, then not.
- **B8 The Locksmith's Mourning.** A room full of keys hung on wire, each one humming. Fussy, melancholic.
- **B9 The Kiln of Last Meals.** A vast brick oven kept warm, with something baking. Domestic, ominous.
- **B10 The Rope Cathedral.** Hundreds of rope ladders and walkways strung across a hollow the height of a tower. Airy, risky, a fine place for a fall.
- **B11 The Undersea Parlour.** A plush drawing room at the bottom of a dry lake, fireplace lit, no one home. Cosy, deeply suspicious.
- **B12 The Drainage Shrine.** An altar in the sewers dedicated to a god of leaks. Squalid, sincere, funny.

### C. Complications
- **C1** The only bridge is occupied, and its occupant wants a toll in something the party doesn't know it has.
- **C2** A rope, rope-bridge or torch was cut earlier by a previous party. The cut ends are fresh.
- **C3** Someone in the party is recognised by something in the dark as "the one who owes."
- **C4** The lamps burn blue and the party can only see things that are lying.
- **C5** An NPC from the town is down here, alive, and utterly lost, and slows everyone down.
- **C6** The relic is hungry and starts negotiating.
- **C7** A monster is sitting, waiting, polite, and has an offer.
- **C8** The floor has a ticket attached: "REPOSSESSION DATE: TODAY."
- **C9** Another party's survivors beg for help, and are not entirely telling the truth.
- **C10** The room's door has locked behind the party and only opens to a particular lie.
- **C11** Someone eats something they should not have, on a dare.
- **C12** The map in the party's pocket is accurate for a different floor.
- **C13** The rope, ledge or ladder was built for the Lienholders, who were shorter, or taller, or both.

### D. Twists and Reveals
- **D1** The monster is guarding the treasure for its own protection, not the party's, and is not the villain.
- **D2** The ally the party trusted was already on the Roll of the Gone.
- **D3** The relic is not cursed, it is merely very, very lonely.
- **D4** The "dead adventurer" on the ledge is a carefully dressed decoy, left by something clever.
- **D5** The room's treasure is a receipt: the party has already been paid for what they're about to do.
- **D6** The tale-teller's story is true, and she was the one it happened to.
- **D7** The feared beast is a youngling, and its parent is about to enter.
- **D8** The shrine, trap or puzzle was built by a Crowhithe local last year, as a joke or a scam.
- **D9** The cursed party member's curse is the only thing keeping the party alive.
- **D10** The floor below has been dug up from the other direction.
- **D11** The thing everyone calls a ghost is a worker still doing a shift.
- **D12** The vault's lamps were lit recently, by someone who left their lunch.

### E. Relics and Ancient Weapons
Format: *Name. Benefit / Catch / Story hook.*

- **E1 The Overdraft Blade.** Hits harder than any steel should / the damage is "borrowed" and shows up later on the wielder / the previous owner's creditors have turned up with a bag.
- **E2 Mother Fennick's Ladle.** Heals a wound by stirring / only works if the healer eats some of the soup / the soup is always the same flavour as a dead person's cooking.
- **E3 Crowbar of the Reasonable Man.** Opens any door politely / refuses to hit anything that has said please / a goblin has said please every time since.
- **E4 The Candlemaker's Crown.** Provides light and reveals hidden things / the light attracts every living thing within a long walk / was worn by a girl who just wanted to read.
- **E5 Dolly Marrow's Sling.** Never misses a sleeping target / weeps loudly when it kills a child's toy / the toy was a mimic.
- **E6 The Ninth Key.** Opens one lock on every layer / each opening costs one memory of the wielder's / it already took the name of the first owner's wife.
- **E7 Loaner's Gauntlet.** Grips anything and never drops it / cannot let go of anything, including a monster, a rope, or a handshake / sends a polite note after each use.
- **E8 The Bell of Small Mercies.** Rung once, an enemy loses its nerve / rung twice, the party loses its own / was cast from forty apologies.
- **E9 A Pair of Tiny Boots.** Walk silently over any floor / they won't be worn by anyone over a particular age / their original owner is still looking for them.
- **E10 The Honest Axe.** Cuts through anything it's told the truth about / sulks if lied to / the party's liar has no hands left, figuratively.
- **E11 Saint Orrel's Ledger.** Writes down an enemy's name, which weakens it / cannot be closed once opened / has started writing the party's names too.
- **E12 The Sunny Lantern.** Its light is warm and tastes of summer / undead are drawn to it like cats / a very old lich keeps one of these as a pet.

### F. Monster Behaviours and Quirks
Simple, visible in combat, fits classic Western monsters.

- **F1 Skeletons** that politely line up and take turns, until you break the line.
- **F2 Slimes** that eat the shiny thing first, then the rest of the pack, then the shiny thing again.
- **F3 Orcs** who flee when their drummer dies and return with a bigger drum.
- **F4 Ghouls** who stop mid-fight to eat whichever of their friends has just fallen.
- **F5 Wraiths** who whisper the names of the dead party members as they strike.
- **F6 A dragon** that hoards things nobody wants: keys, shoes, one specific brand of boot-polish.
- **F7 Demons** who lawyer: they will negotiate their own defeat for terms.
- **F8 Giant rats** that carry off one item from the pack in the first round and are never seen again.
- **F9 Mimics** shaped as small, lovely things, who have developed taste.
- **F10 Bone-wolves** that circle forever, waiting for a gap that will not come until the party loses its nerve.
- **F11 Gargoyles** that are always still when watched, and always closer when not.
- **F12 A troll** that dozes mid-battle if left alone for a few turns, and wakes cross.
- **F13 Cultists** who chant the wrong spell, hopefully, cheerfully, and very loud.
- **F14 Ancient golems** that follow orders given by anyone, including the party, including accidentally.

### G. Outcomes, by Tone
**Funny**
- **GF1** The party's most valuable treasure is stolen by a rat. The rat is now a hero in the town.
- **GF2** A minor curse makes one member say everything as a complaint. It is a good morale tool.
- **GF3** A fight is won because a skeleton insisted on shaking hands first.
- **GF4** The dragon gets a gift of a boot-polish tin and falls asleep, enchanted. Nobody mentions it to the Wardens.
- **GF5** The temple's raise-the-dead rite works perfectly, on the wrong body.

**Sad**
- **GS1** The party wins and finds the monster was wearing a child's coat.
- **GS2** A hero is raised from the dead, and no longer recognises her party.
- **GS3** The last tally mark on a wall bears a name the party knows.
- **GS4** An NPC finds the thing they came for, and it is only a bone.
- **GS5** A long-awaited reunion is with a ghost who has forgotten how to answer.

**Uplifting**
- **GU1** A party carries a wounded troll to the surface and the tavern adopts it.
- **GU2** A failed raising is followed by a second, better one, paid for by strangers.
- **GU3** The sneak, the thief and the liar do one honest thing, together.
- **GU4** A creature that has been afraid for centuries is finally told it is allowed to leave.
- **GU5** The town stops the dawn reading for one morning, because someone came back.

**Memorable**
- **GM1** The party's only survivor walks out carrying all six sets of equipment, none of them hers.
- **GM2** A whole floor rearranges itself around a single, deafening sneeze.
- **GM3** The party dies, and the next party finds them, and robs them politely.
- **GM4** A name is added to the Roll of the Gone in the party's own handwriting, a day before they leave.
- **GM5** A bell rings in the deep, and the creatures all turn and face the surface.

## 3. Recipes

Formula: *Ingredient + Ingredient + Ingredient, with a tone.*

**R1 (Funny).** B5 Weighing Bridge + F6 dragon that hoards junk + E3 Crowbar of the Reasonable Man + GF4.
*Worked example.* The balance in the middle of the bridge tips against the heaviest member of the party, who has been carrying the dragon's hoard of boots. The dragon wants them back. The Crowbar refuses to hit it, because it said "if you please." Everyone leaves the bridge in the right order, with all their boots.

**R2 (Funny).** A7 Gudrun Sixfingers + C11 dare + B9 Kiln of Last Meals + GF2.
*Worked example.* Gudrun eats the thing in the oven on a dare. Nothing happens for two floors, then she begins to speak only in complaints about the soup. The party grows to rely on her: she is never wrong about the soup.

**R3 (Sad).** A11 Joss the Sweeper + B3 Drop of Names + D2 ally already on the Roll + GS3.
*Worked example.* Joss gives the party a jar of ash and asks them to pour it at the edge. On the wall is a tally scratched in a hand the party knows. The name on it is the ally's, written long before this expedition began. Nobody speaks. They pour the ash.

**R4 (Sad).** A6 Perrin Lowe + C7 polite monster with an offer + D7 youngling + GS1.
*Worked example.* The ghoul in the crypt offers the party a deal: leave the nursery alone and it will share what it knows. Perrin apologises as the party kills it. Afterwards they find the coat, a child's, in its nest, and Perrin stops healing for a while.

**R5 (Uplifting).** A5 Old Tamsin Quell + B11 Undersea Parlour + D6 tale is true + GU4.
*Worked example.* The fireplace is lit and the chair is her mother's. Tamsin finishes the story she could not end, to an empty room, and something in the dark lets go of her. The party walks out with a woman ten years younger in the face.

**R6 (Uplifting).** A13 Brother Kesk + C5 lost townsfolk + D11 ghost worker + GU5.
*Worked example.* Kesk finds a clerk who has been stuck down here with a pickaxe since before the Roll began. The clerk is led to the surface and counted. The Wardens, caught off guard, strike Kesk's name off the Roll and put the clerk's on a short, happy page.

**R7 (Memorable).** B10 Rope Cathedral + F14 golem obeys anyone + C2 cut rope + GM2.
*Worked example.* The party's thief shouts "let go!" to the golem holding the walkway. It lets go. The whole cathedral swings and the party's only survivor is the one who was already falling.

**R8 (Memorable).** E11 Saint Orrel's Ledger + A9 Corporal Moss + D5 treasure is a receipt + GM4.
*Worked example.* The Ledger writes the party's names in a column. In the morning Moss reads them from the Roll, a day early, in the party's own handwriting. It is correct, in the end.

## 4. Tales and Rumours

Each ends in a hook. Tone is marked.

**T1 (Funny). Told by Maud the Lender.**
"A fellow came in with a sword that screamed. I said I'd give him three silver for it. He said it was worth three hundred, so I held it up to the lamp and it said, loud as a bell, 'He owes me forty.' I paid him nothing. The Overdraft Blade is in the Pawn Gallery, third case, and it wants a new owner with good credit."
*Hook: B6 Pawn Gallery, E1 Overdraft Blade.*

**T2 (Sad). Told by Old Tamsin Quell.**
"I had a daughter, Ria, who went down the Tallow Stair with a candle and a book. She came up in three pieces, which is the number the Wardens like. But I counted: there was a fourth. Her book was not in any of them. Find me the book. I'll tell you what is in it if you bring it back."
*Hook: B1 Tallow Stair, a lost book.*

**T3 (Uplifting). Told by Sister Ottoline.**
"Three weeks ago a man came in with an urn he had carried from the deep. Not his own. A stranger's. He had gone out of his way, down seven stairs, to bring a stranger's ash home. We raised her, no charge. She walked out of here and shook his hand. Find the man, he is still in town, and ask him what else he's carried."
*Hook: A recruit, a hero, the Drop of Names.*

**T4 (Memorable). Told by Brannoch.**
"I went down once. Reached the Receiving Hall. Table forty paces long, set for guests. A place card with my name on it. I'd never told anyone my real name was Dennis. I left my sword on the table and ran. The place cards are still there. Yours might be too."
*Hook: B2 Receiving Hall, place cards.*

**T5 (Funny). Told by Hobb Ferrule.**
"Sold a boy a sword last month. He said, 'Does it come with a guarantee?' I said, 'It comes with a name.' It's called Regret. He took it down to the Choir Cistern and a mermaid hit him with a lute. Don't ask about the lute. Ask about the mermaid."
*Hook: B4 Choir Cistern, the mermaid with the lute.*

**T6 (Sad). Told by Corporal Moss.**
"I read out a name this morning that I'd read out eleven years ago. Same name. Same spelling. Either the dead are coming back and dying again, or someone's using the name. I'd like to know which. I'd prefer the first. The second is worse. Go to the Drop of Names and check the wall."
*Hook: B3 Drop of Names, a reused name.*

**T7 (Uplifting). Told by Wenna Ashby.**
"Had a girl stay here once, scared stiff. Said she was a thief, didn't want to go down. I said, 'Sleep. We'll see.' She went down the next morning and came back a week later with a bag of keys and a limp, and paid for the room twice. She keeps the Ninth Key in a sock. She'll tell you where it goes if you buy the ale."
*Hook: E6 Ninth Key, B8 Locksmith's Mourning.*

**T8 (Memorable). Told by Merrow Dunn, a candle seller.**
"Light the fat candle on the fourth stair, and sing. Don't ask why. The Quiet Below will ask whether you've come about the account. Say yes. Say it as if you mean it. People who said no don't have names any more. People who said yes had a very pleasant afternoon."
*Hook: The Quiet Below, the Tally-Singers.*

**T9 (Funny). Told by Pim the tavern boy.**
"Right, so, the Slow Orchard. There's a tree with fruit that never ripens, and my uncle said if you pee on it, it ripens. He did. It did. It was extremely ripe. It chased him for three floors. That's all I know. Please don't tell my mother."
*Hook: B7 Slow Orchard, the ripening trees.*

**T10 (Sad). Told by Joss the Sweeper.**
"I keep a jar for every one. When the jar is full, I pour it out the back door, into the river. Last week I opened a jar and it was warm. Not the jar. The ash. It had a little pulse. I don't think that's allowed. The Sisters won't talk about it. Take it down, if you can bear it."
*Hook: A warm jar of ash, the Drainage Shrine.*

**T11 (Uplifting). Told by Gudrun Sixfingers.**
"I was born with a bread knife and a bad attitude. I killed a gargoyle with the knife because I was too stubborn to run. It was crying afterwards. Not me, the gargoyle. I buried it in the Rope Cathedral, in a hole it had dug. Someone should put a flower there. It would be nice."
*Hook: B10 Rope Cathedral, F11 gargoyle's grave.*

**T12 (Memorable). Told by Maud the Lender.**
"Everyone who goes down gets a ticket. You won't see it, but it's pinned somewhere on you. When I weigh you, I can feel it. Yours says, 'Due: soon.' Don't look so surprised. Everyone's does. Mine says 'Overdue.' Go find out what it means before you find out the hard way."
*Hook: C8 repossession ticket, the secret of the debt.*

## 5. Event Seeds

**V1. The Weighing Bridge Tips.**
*Trigger:* the party crosses with more loot than it came in with.
*What happens:* the central balance tilts and tips everyone toward the drop.
*Trace:* the party's own pack. The balance was tuned to the Lienholders' idea of "fair."
*Consequence:* an item must be dropped; the heaviest is chosen by the bridge.
*Chronicle:* "{name} lost {item} to the balance at {place}, which found it too heavy for a fair price."

**V2. The Choir Floods.**
*Trigger:* a bell is rung in the Choir Cistern.
*What happens:* the water rises, singing, and the pipes pitch up into a scream.
*Trace:* the ringing disturbed the old pressure valves.
*Consequence:* the party is cut off from the stairs, and must use the other exit.
*Chronicle:* "At {place}, the party rang a bell and the water answered."

**V3. The Orc Drummers Meet the Ghouls.**
*Trigger:* the party fights near a ghoul feeding-ground.
*What happens:* the drums wake the ghouls, who attack the orcs.
*Trace:* drums are heard before the battle; scraps of orc armour litter the floor.
*Consequence:* the party can slip past, or loot the aftermath.
*Chronicle:* "{monster} and {monster} killed each other at {place}. The party took the credit and the boots."

**V4. The Hungry Relic.**
*Trigger:* a relic is unused for a long time.
*What happens:* it starts eating the party's other possessions, one by one.
*Trace:* each item gone is within arm's reach of the relic.
*Consequence:* the party must feed it or part with it.
*Chronicle:* "{item} ate {item2} in the night. {name} swears it said grace first."

**V5. The Rope Cathedral Unravels.**
*Trigger:* a cut rope is left unreported on the way down.
*What happens:* a walkway drops on the return trip.
*Trace:* the fresh cut ends, seen on the way in.
*Consequence:* the party must find another way up, or climb.
*Chronicle:* "{place} gave way on the way home. The cut was visible on the way in. {name} had looked at it."

**V6. The Kiln Opens.**
*Trigger:* the party rests within hearing of the Kiln of Last Meals.
*What happens:* something is taken out, and it is for them.
*Trace:* the smell of bread, noticed hours before.
*Consequence:* those who eat are healed; those who refuse, the Kiln remembers.
*Chronicle:* "{name} ate the bread at {place}. It was good. That is the worst thing about it."

**V7. The Gargoyles Move.**
*Trigger:* the party leaves a gargoyle unwatched while arguing.
*What happens:* it arrives where the party's weakest member stands.
*Trace:* scrape marks on the floor, and a missing statue in the corner.
*Consequence:* a hard fight in a bad spot.
*Chronicle:* "{name} turned their back on {monster}. It was unreasonable of them."

**V8. The Demon's Terms.**
*Trigger:* a demon is lowered below half its strength.
*What happens:* it offers to leave for a price, written on parchment.
*Trace:* the contract is real, and the signature is a party member's, from earlier.
*Consequence:* the party can sign, or kill it and lose something else.
*Chronicle:* "{monster} offered terms at {place}. {name} read the small print and did not like it."

**V9. The Slow Orchard Ripens.**
*Trigger:* the party brings something dead into the Orchard.
*What happens:* the fruit swells and falls, and something inside it moves.
*Trace:* the body, left in the soil.
*Consequence:* the fruit is food or a monster, depending on who ate.
*Chronicle:* "Something grew from {name}'s friend at {place}. It was not the friend."

**V10. The Roll Calls the Party.**
*Trigger:* the party's survivors are long overdue.
*What happens:* the Wardens enter them into the Roll, and the walls of the deep answer by reading the same names.
*Trace:* the same names, spoken at once on every floor.
*Consequence:* everything in the Vault knows the party is "gone" and treats them accordingly.
*Chronicle:* "The Wardens named the party dead at dawn. By noon the Vault agreed."

## 7. Discovery Hooks

**H1 The Lienholders' Primer on Loans.** Found chiselled on a wall in the Receiving Hall. *Why it exists:* the Lienholders taught their apprentices to borrow against the future. *Build:* a spellcaster who casts from tomorrow's reserves, strong now and increasingly weak later, rewarding careful timing.

**H2 The Overdraft Blade (E1).** *Why it exists:* the first Lienholder commander could not pay his army and paid them in damage instead. *Build:* a fighter who swings freely early in the delve and plays a long, careful game on the way back, because every blow is a debt.

**H3 Mother Fennick's Ladle (E2).** *Why it exists:* a soup-kitchen for the workers, kept going centuries after the workers died. *Build:* a healer who must eat a share of each cure, rewarding a party that carries real food and a stomach that can take it.

**H4 The Tally-Singers' Hymnal.** Found in the hands of a skeleton in the Drainage Shrine. *Why it exists:* the cult's founder wrote the songs that keep the count. *Build:* a bard-like support who sings the party's losses back as strength, turning each death into a benefit for the survivors.

**H5 The Ninth Key (E6).** *Why it exists:* it was the Lienholders' master key to the vault they built inside the vault. *Build:* a thief who opens shortcuts at the cost of memory, who must keep a journal to remember who she is.

**H6 Candlemaker's Crown (E4).** *Why it exists:* a child's reading light, enchanted by a grieving father. *Build:* a scout whose party sees everything and is seen by everything, a high-risk lantern build for sharp tacticians.

**H7 The Dry Treatise on Bones.** Found in a Pawn Gallery case. *Why it exists:* a dissenting Candler tried to catalogue the skeletal dead by their manners. *Build:* a necromancer who bargains rather than commands, who courts the dead by etiquette and gains followers for politeness.

**H8 The Honest Axe (E10).** *Why it exists:* forged by a judge who wanted a blade that would not lie in court. *Build:* a fighter who must tell the truth to the party and enemies alike, rewarding a role-played, plain-spoken hero.

**H9 Saint Orrel's Ledger (E11).** *Why it exists:* a saint tried to write down everything the Vault owed and ran out of pages. *Build:* a scholar who weakens enemies by writing their names down, a slow, patient, book-keeping way to win battles.

**H10 The Quiet's Receipt.** Given by the Quiet Below to a party that says "yes." *Why it exists:* the creditor acknowledges payments. *Build:* a party-wide pact that turns the dead into installments, so that each death makes the next survivors stronger, at a cost the player must read in the small print and decide whether they are willing to pay.
